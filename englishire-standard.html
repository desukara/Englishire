A Published Statement of Practice

The Englishire Standard

Six principles for responsible temporary English teacher cover.

This Standard explains how Englishire approaches review, confirmation, preparation, classroom conduct and communication when supporting schools across Tokyo.

Preamble

An absence may alter the timetable. It need not diminish the school day.

A teacher’s absence should never diminish a school’s commitment to learning.

Professional cover exists to protect continuity. It is not merely the placement of an available adult, but the considered provision of a teacher who understands the assignment, arrives prepared and respects the school already in progress.

This Standard sets out the principles Englishire applies to temporary English teacher cover in Tokyo. It recognises that urgent circumstances may limit what is known or possible, while maintaining that suitability, honesty, safeguarding and professional conduct do not become optional when time is short.

The objective is straightforward: to help schools preserve continuity, uphold reasonable expectations and make sound staffing decisions without allowing the immediate timetable to force an unsuitable promise.

Scope of this Standard

This is Englishire’s operating statement of practice. It guides how assignments are reviewed and supported, but it is not a contract, guarantee of availability or substitute for the terms confirmed for a particular assignment.

Principle I

Continuity Is the Purpose

Teacher cover exists to protect the continuity of education, not merely to occupy a classroom.

A temporary lesson sits within an existing course, timetable and school culture. The work completed today affects what the learner and permanent teacher can do next. Cover should therefore preserve purposeful learning, established expectations and the calm order of the school day as far as circumstances reasonably allow.

Continuity does not require imitation or theatrical novelty. It requires a teacher to understand what has already been built, conduct the lesson with proportionate confidence and leave an accurate handover that allows the course to continue without avoidable confusion.

In practice

  • Follow the school’s lesson sequence and classroom expectations.
  • Record useful information about work completed and matters requiring attention.
  • As an assignment lengthens, discuss planning, assessment and wider duties explicitly.

Principle II

Understand Before Confirming

An assignment should not be accepted before its essential requirements are understood.

Urgency is common in staffing enquiries, but it is not a reason to abandon judgement. Dates, location, reporting time, learner groups, lesson format, travel, preparation and responsibilities beyond teaching can all determine whether the work is feasible.

Not every detail will be available at first contact. The standard is not perfect information; it is a clear distinction between what is known, what remains provisional and what must be resolved before confirmation.

Before confirmation, establish

  • The location, dates, reporting time, timetable and expected finish.
  • The learner ages, approximate levels, class sizes and lesson purpose.
  • The materials, preparation available and any additional duties.
  • The school contact, arrival arrangements and relevant procedures.

Principle III

Suitability Before Availability

The fact that a teacher is available does not, by itself, make that teacher suitable.

Professional selection concerns fit. Age group, lesson demands, school setting, practical experience, temperament and the ability to follow an established brief all matter. English teaching across children’s programmes, examination courses, adult classes and international settings is not interchangeable simply because the subject shares a name.

A suitable teacher listens carefully, accepts guidance, exercises proportionate authority and recognises when a matter should be referred rather than resolved independently. A measured acknowledgement of limits is generally more useful than conspicuous confidence.

Suitability includes

  • Relevant experience with the learners and lesson type.
  • Practical ability to meet the timetable and travel requirements.
  • Temperament appropriate to the school and circumstances.
  • Discretion, reliability and willingness to work within the brief.

Principle IV

Preparation Before Placement

Professional teaching begins before the classroom door opens.

Preparation reduces uncertainty. A teacher who knows where to report, what is to be taught, which materials are available and whom to contact is better placed to begin calmly and preserve that calm for learners.

Preparation is shared work. The school provides the information reasonably available; the teacher reads it, seeks clarification where necessary and arrives ready to act upon it. Where information is missing, that absence should be stated rather than disguised by assumption.

A sound briefing ordinarily addresses

  • Arrival, reception, identification and the principal school contact.
  • Timetable, rooms, transitions, breaks and finish arrangements.
  • Lesson objectives, resources, technology and expected outcomes.
  • Classroom routines, communication procedures and relevant learner considerations.
  • What should be recorded or handed over at the end of the assignment.

Principle V

Professional Conduct and Respect

Professional conduct includes respect for the school already in progress.

Punctuality, suitable presentation, attentive communication, confidentiality and composed classroom conduct are the ordinary foundations of work undertaken on behalf of a school. A temporary teacher enters an established community and should do so with attentiveness rather than presumption.

Schools differ in their routines, language, hierarchy, shared spaces and expectations. The teacher contributes professional judgement, but the school remains the principal authority for its policies and educational priorities. Questions should be asked courteously, and unfamiliar practice should not be mistaken for deficiency.

Professionalism is generally quiet

  • Follow established procedures unless safety or professional duty requires otherwise.
  • Handle school and learner information with discretion.
  • Communicate difficulties accurately and without unnecessary drama.
  • Support the institution rather than making the temporary teacher its centre of attention.

Principle VI

Honest Communication and Responsible Restraint

Urgency may alter what is possible. It does not alter what is acceptable.

Temporary assignments change. A timetable may be revised, an absent teacher may return, or a single day may become a longer requirement. Material changes should be communicated early, plainly and to the people responsible for acting upon them. Availability, duties and suitability should not be assumed to remain unchanged.

Honest communication may produce a conditional answer, a request for further information or, on occasion, a refusal. Safeguarding, suitability and truthful confirmation remain essential. Englishire will not promise universal availability, an impossible response time or a particular arrangement before the relevant circumstances have been considered.

Responsible restraint means

  • Do not describe an assignment as confirmed while an essential condition remains unresolved.
  • Do not disguise uncertainty beneath a convenient reassurance.
  • Review material changes rather than presuming the original agreement still applies.
  • Decline work that cannot be undertaken suitably, punctually or responsibly.

Applying the Standard Over Time

Temporary cover should help the school manage the present without losing sight of what follows.

Repeated absence, a long recruitment process, timetable growth or recurring specialist needs may indicate that a broader contingency plan is required. Temporary cover can provide breathing room, but should not become a chain of hurried arrangements where a more deliberate decision is needed.

Where a relationship continues, each assignment should improve understanding. School routines become clearer, briefing becomes more efficient and future decisions can be made with better information. Schools are therefore welcome to introduce themselves before an immediate absence arises.

Closing Declaration

The standard, stated plainly.

Every assignment deserves proper consideration. Every school deserves honest communication. Every classroom deserves a teacher who arrives prepared to uphold the trust placed in them.

This is the standard Englishire seeks to apply: continuity before convenience, suitability before availability, preparation before placement, and professional judgement throughout.
